Detailed Description

Chord patterns, spelled chords, inversions and the reviewed chord catalog.

MCC::ChordPattern stores up to seven tones within two octaves as diatonic steps and semitones above the root, built at compile time from a chord formula such as "1 3 5 b7 9". MCC::Chord spells a pattern from a root MCC::NoteName and voices its inversions in close position. MCC::Chords holds the named catalog with symbols in program memory, read through Foundation::Utils::Flash, and MCC::Chords::FromScale() lists every catalog chord whose tones are written in a scale, as the historical ChordPool did. See Music-Domain Specification (SPEC-CHD-*).

const MCC::Chord e9 =
MCC::Chords::Make(MCC::NoteName(MCC::Letter::E), MCC::Chords::Id::DominantNinth);
MCC::NoteName ninth = e9.ToneAt(5); // F#
MCC::Chords::ScaleChord pool[64];
size_t count = MCC::Chords::FromScale(cMajorScale, pool, 64);

Catalog and Provenance

Identifiers are stable. Entries 0-22 come from the historical OLD MCC .Chord files under ChordFlash/ChordDefinitions, read with their interval lists and modifiers applied; the Arduino copy and the older MusicCompositionCore copy carry the same entries. Their NoteLayout inversion masks were copied between chords and were not migrated: inversions are computed instead. Spellings are shown from C.

Names in parentheses are aliases accepted by MCC::Chords::Find(). ChordCatalogTests checks this table, uniqueness of identifiers, names, symbols, aliases and patterns, and spelling from all 35 roots with up to two accidentals. On AVR the catalog is emitted in .progmem.data.

Recognition of a chord from loose notes is not part of this module yet: a pitch-class set is often ambiguous (C6 and Am7 share their notes) and loses spelling, so it will be designed with the MIDILAR integration.
